\noindent
This note collects, in one place and with terms defined, a set of
results and observations about the \emph{static} (time-independent)
linearised response of a self-gravitating body containing an inviscid
fluid region --- a fluid core --- whose stratification is not neutral.
The short version:

\begin{enumerate}
\item If the fluid's squared buoyancy frequency $N^2$ is nonzero, the
  static problem posed with the fluid as an elastic material (the
  natural reading of ``set the time derivatives to zero'') has
  \emph{no solution}: its field equations force the fluid's radial
  displacement to ride the equipotentials, and that assignment is
  generically incompatible with the kinematic matching at the
  core--mantle boundary (\S\ref{sec:slaving}--\ref{sec:nonexist}).
\item This failure is invariant under the fluid-relabelling symmetry:
  no choice of gauge representative repairs it. The static problem is
  well posed on the quotient by the relabelling group \emph{exactly}
  when $N^2 = 0$, where the group is large enough to absorb the
  offending degree of freedom (\S\ref{sec:gauge}).
\item Dahlen's classical treatment of the fluid is immune because it
  eliminates the fluid displacement before the conflict can arise. The
  price, usually left implicit, is a normal-displacement discontinuity
  at the core--mantle boundary, carried in the formalism as a surface
  mass density. Dahlen's static problem is well posed and its answer
  is independent of the fluid's bulk modulus at every degree
  $\ell \ge 1$ (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}).
\item Dissipation decides. Every physical regularisation we can
  identify --- fluid viscosity, diffusion of the stratifying quantity
  (a point made by Wunsch in the 1970s), or a Maxwell viscoelastic core
  --- yields a well-posed evolution whose $t \to \infty$ limit is the
  Dahlen (secular) answer. Dahlen's closure is therefore not one
  arbitrary resolution among many: it is the distinguished limit of
  every dissipative completion (\S\ref{sec:regular}).
\item For glacial-isostatic-adjustment timescales the body sits
  extraordinarily deep in the secular regime --- even a core with
  $N^2$ six orders of magnitude below plausible values has buoyancy
  periods of years against loading periods of millennia --- so the
  Dahlen closure is the physically correct static limit there, and the
  entire ambiguity quantified below is practically negligible for GIA
  next to, say, three-dimensional viscosity variations. The interest
  of the result is conceptual, and for bodies or observables where
  core stratification itself is the target (\S\ref{sec:persp}).
\item The dissipative selection is also the \emph{method}: making the fluid regions an artificial Maxwell
  solid, applying the load as a step, and relaxing until the
  \emph{solid} stops moving computes the secular response with
  nothing but welded elastic solves --- no fluid machinery, no slip
  interface, no kernel handling beyond rigid modes, on any geometry.
  Implemented generically in the problem layer, and \emph{cheaper}
  in three dimensions than the welded static solve it replaces
  (\S\ref{sec:maxwell}).
\item The formulations are ranked accordingly. Free slip is the
  $\eta \to 0$ asymptotics of a viscous boundary layer --- correct
  about the interface once the layer has collapsed, silent about the
  interior physics that collapses it --- and the measurements show
  the slip classes wandering in the same $N^2$ band as welded: both
  are finite idealisations of the dissipative completion. The
  relaxation is the formulation-faithful computation; Dahlen is its
  converged spherical closure; welded and slip are its
  approximants, retained as instruments (\S\ref{sec:persp}).
\item \emph{Under review (added 21 October 2026).} Dahlen's closure
  appears to generalise beyond the spherical background: for a
  hydrostatic fluid region under arbitrary solids the free function
  of the tangential balance is a one-dimensional unknown on the level
  foliation, pinned by mass conservation below each level surface, and
  the same equation decides degree 0. A non-level wall changes the
  gauge class, and the sphere is the singular limit in which the
  transverse matching collapses to Dahlen's surface mass. The
  derivation, with its derived, argued and conjectured steps marked,
  is \S\ref{sec:general}; the measured degree-1 offsets of the Maxwell
  route on stably stratified cores that prompted it are recorded there.
  Items 4--6 and the hierarchy below stand until its open steps are
  settled.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\textwidth}{\small\textbf{Method hierarchy.}
For coupled elasto-gravity problems with fluid regions: the Maxwell
relaxation of the fluid (\code{SetFluid}'s default treatment) is the
general method --- any geometry, any stratification, any number of
regions. The gauge-penalty treatment is clean only for neutrally
stratified fluids: at $N^2 \neq 0$ its semi-convergence is
Rayleigh--Taylor growth, and answers are defined only to the
stratification band. For hydrostatic (spherical) models Dahlen's
mixed formulation is the practical workhorse. The slipping and welded
formulations are finite idealisations of the dissipative completion
and serve as instruments.}}
\end{center}

\section{Setting and definitions}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Background state.} A non-rotating body occupies
$B \subset \R^d$ ($d = 2$ or $3$; the two-dimensional ``gravitating
disc'' is the testing geometry of this library and all statements
below hold in both dimensions). It consists of a solid region and a
fluid region separated by an interface $\Sigma$ (for concreteness, a
fluid core of radius $c$ under a solid mantle; the core--mantle
boundary, CMB). The background is a radial hydrostatic equilibrium:
density $\rho(r)$, gravitational potential $\Phi_0$ with
$\nabla^2\Phi_0 = 4\pi G \rho$ and $g(r) = \Phi_0'(r) \ge 0$, and
pressure $p^0$ with $\mathrm{d}p^0/\mathrm{d}r = -\rho g$.

\paragraph{The fluid, and $N^2$.} The fluid is \emph{inviscid} and
\emph{barotropic-elastic}: its strain energy per unit referential
volume is $V(\mathbf{x}, J)$, a function of position and of the
volume ratio $J = \det F$ alone, with bulk modulus
$\kappa = J^2 \partial^2_J V$ evaluated at equilibrium. It supports
no shear stress. The \emph{squared buoyancy frequency} is
\begin{equation}
  N^2 \;=\; -g\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}
            + \frac{g\rho}{\kappa}\right),
  \label{eq:N2}
\end{equation}
the frequency (squared) of vertical oscillation of a displaced fluid
parcel. $N^2 > 0$ is stable stratification, $N^2 < 0$ unstable, and
$N^2 = 0$ --- the \emph{Adams--Williamson} or \emph{neutral} state ---
means the density profile is exactly the one produced by adiabatic
self-compression, so a displaced parcel is always neutrally buoyant.
The library's test model \code{fluid\_core} (uniform density,
finite $\kappa$) has $N^2 = -g^2\rho/\kappa < 0$; its constructed
twin \code{aw\_core} has $N^2 = 0$ identically, by choosing the
density profile first and defining
$\kappa = -\rho^2 g / (\mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}r)$ in closed form.

\paragraph{Perturbations.} Linearise about the background:
$\bu$ is the (referential) displacement field, $\phi$ the
\emph{Eulerian} perturbation of the gravitational potential (the
change at a fixed spatial point), and, in the fluid,
$p^1$ and $\rho^1$ the Eulerian pressure and density perturbations.
The \emph{Lagrangian} pressure perturbation (the change following the
material) is $\Delta p = p^1 + \bu\cdot\nabla p^0$; for the
barotropic-elastic fluid $\Delta p = -\kappa\,\Div\bu$. Mass
conservation gives $\rho^1 = -\Div(\rho\,\bu)$.

\paragraph{The three formulations compared.} All act on the same
background and load; they differ in how the fluid and the interface
are described. (Precise weak forms:
\code{doc/quasi\_static\_models.tex} and
\code{doc/gravitating\_elasticity.md}; here only what matters.)
\begin{description}
\item[welded:] the fluid is kept as the elastic material above, and
  the displacement is a single continuous field across $\Sigma$ ---
  both normal and tangential components match.
\item[slip (slipping interface):] the fluid displacement is an
  independent field; only the \emph{normal} component is matched at
  $\Sigma$ ($\bn\cdot\jump{\bu} = 0$, in the notation of
  \code{doc/slip\_interface.tex}); the tangential jump --- the slip
  --- is free, and the interface carries the corresponding pressure
  and gravity forms.
\item[Dahlen:] the fluid displacement is eliminated analytically
  (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}); the fluid contributes a volume term in $\phi$
  and interface terms at $\Sigma$, and the solid is described in the
  standard (effective-moduli) variables.
\end{description}

\paragraph{The fluid-relabelling (FRL) symmetry.} A
\emph{relabelling} is an infinitesimal rearrangement $\bw$ of the
fluid's material labels that leaves every physical field unchanged:
it must preserve the mass distribution and whatever else the energy
depends on, and must not move the boundary,
$\bw\cdot\bn = 0$ on $\partial(\text{fluid})$. Which $\bw$ qualify
depends on the stratification:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{strict class} ($N^2 \ne 0$): $\Div\bw = 0$ and
  $\bw\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = 0$ --- volume-preserving and tangent to the
  level surfaces of the background potential. On a spherical
  (circular) interface the induced boundary motions are the
  surface-divergence-free tangential fields; in two dimensions,
  rigid rotations only.
\item \textbf{class K} ($N^2 = 0$): $\Div(\rho\,\bw) = 0$ with
  $\bw\cdot\bn = 0$ --- the ``trivial displacements''. This is a much
  larger group; in particular every tangential boundary field is the
  trace of some member, so every slip is removable
  (\code{doc/gravitating\_elasticity.md} \S5; the measured
  adjudication is in \S\ref{sec:evidence}).
\end{itemize}
Solutions of the static equations, when they exist, are determined
only up to the relevant class: the physically meaningful object is
the equivalence class (the \emph{orbit}), and any gauge-invariant
observable --- surface displacement, the potential, tractions --- is
constant on it. The Maitra--Al-Attar programme implemented in this
library enforces normal continuity, leaves the slip free, and selects
a representative of the orbit by a gauge condition (in practice a
small penalty, iteratively refined); see \S\ref{sec:gauge} for when
this is and is not legitimate.

\section{The interior slaving}
\label{sec:slaving}

\emph{Status: derived.} Work on the radial background, in either
dimension, at one harmonic degree $\ell$: every perturbation is a
radial function times one harmonic $Y$ (a spherical harmonic
$Y_{\ell m}$ in three dimensions, $\cos\ell\theta$ or $\sin\ell\theta$
in two), and the equations below are statements about those radial
functions. Every line of the derivation is written out.

\paragraph{Conventions.} Those of \S\ref{sec:setting}, used without
change throughout. Primes are radial derivatives,
$\rho' = \mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}r$, and $\hat{\mathbf r}$ is the
outward radial unit vector, $u_r = \bu\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}$. The
background has $g = \Phi_0' \ge 0$, so
\begin{equation}
  \nabla\Phi_0 = g\,\hat{\mathbf r},
  \qquad
  \frac{\mathrm{d}p^0}{\mathrm{d}r} = -\rho g,
  \qquad
  \bu\cdot\nabla p^0 = -\rho g\,u_r .
  \label{eq:conv-background}
\end{equation}
The perturbation $\phi$ is the Eulerian perturbation of the
gravitational potential, with the background's sign: the gravitational
body force on the fluid is $-\rho\nabla(\Phi_0 + \phi)$ to first order,
and $\nabla^2\phi = 4\pi G\rho^1$. The linearised static momentum
balance of the inviscid fluid is then
\begin{equation}
  -\nabla p^1 - \rho\,\nabla\phi - \rho^1\,\nabla\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0 ,
  \label{eq:momentum}
\end{equation}
and its Lagrangian pressure, constitutive law and mass conservation
(\S\ref{sec:setting}) are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta p = p^1 + \bu\cdot\nabla p^0 = p^1 - \rho g\,u_r ,
  \qquad
  \Delta p = -\kappa\,\Div\bu ,
  \qquad
  \rho^1 = -\Div(\rho\,\bu) = -\rho\,\Div\bu - \rho'\,u_r .
  \label{eq:constitutive}
\end{equation}
Write $\nabla_{\!\perp}$ for the part of the gradient tangent to the
spheres $r = \text{const}$ (circles in two dimensions), which are the
level surfaces of $\Phi_0$: $\nabla = \hat{\mathbf r}\,\partial_r +
\nabla_{\!\perp}$, with $\nabla_{\!\perp}\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0$ and
$\nabla_{\!\perp}\rho = \mathbf 0$. Steps 1 and 2 use only
\eqref{eq:momentum}; Step 3 adds \eqref{eq:constitutive}; Step 4 adds
the interface.

\textbf{Step 1 (tangential balance).} The tangential part of
\eqref{eq:momentum} is
\begin{equation}
  -\nabla_{\!\perp} p^1 - \rho\,\nabla_{\!\perp}\phi
  - \rho^1\,\nabla_{\!\perp}\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0 .
\end{equation}
The last term vanishes because $\nabla\Phi_0$ is radial: the
tangential balance has no $\rho^1$-term. Since $\rho$ is constant on
each sphere, $\rho\,\nabla_{\!\perp}\phi =
\nabla_{\!\perp}(\rho\,\phi)$, and so
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\!\perp}\bigl(p^1 + \rho\,\phi\bigr) = \mathbf 0 ,
  \qquad\text{that is}\qquad
  p^1 + \rho\,\phi = f(r) ,
  \label{eq:tangential}
\end{equation}
with $f$ constant on each level surface. At degree $\ell$ the left side
of \eqref{eq:tangential} is a radial function times $Y$; a function
constant on each sphere has no component along a harmonic of degree
$\ell \ge 1$ (its projection is $f(r)\int Y\,\mathrm{d}\Omega = 0$).
Projecting \eqref{eq:tangential} onto $Y$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
  p^1 = -\rho\,\phi \qquad (\ell \ge 1).
  \label{eq:p-slaved}
\end{equation}
At $\ell = 0$ the argument fails at its first line. Every degree-0
field is a function of $r$ alone, $\nabla_{\!\perp}$ annihilates all of
them, and the tangential balance is empty: $p^1 + \rho\,\phi = f(r)$
is left free, a constant on each level surface and so a free function
of radius in each fluid region, which the radial balance and Poisson's
equation relate to $\rho^1$ and $\phi$ without fixing. This is the
degree-0 matter of \S\ref{sec:dahlen}, and the reason everything below
is restricted to $\ell \ge 1$.

\textbf{Step 2 (radial balance).} The radial part of
\eqref{eq:momentum}, with $\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = g$, is
\begin{equation}
  -\partial_r p^1 - \rho\,\partial_r\phi - \rho^1 g = 0 .
  \label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
Substitute \eqref{eq:p-slaved}. Because $\rho$ depends on $r$,
\begin{equation}
  -\partial_r p^1 = \partial_r(\rho\,\phi)
  = \rho'\,\phi + \rho\,\partial_r\phi ,
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:radial} becomes
\begin{equation}
  \rho'\,\phi + \rho\,\partial_r\phi - \rho\,\partial_r\phi
  - \rho^1 g = 0 .
\end{equation}
The two $\rho\,\partial_r\phi$ terms cancel, leaving
$\rho^1 g = \rho'\,\phi$; dividing by $g > 0$ (at $r > 0$),
\begin{equation}
  \rho^1 = \frac{1}{g}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\phi
  \qquad (\ell \ge 1).
  \label{eq:rho-slaved}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:p-slaved}--\eqref{eq:rho-slaved} are exactly
Dahlen's interior relations: they follow from momentum balance alone
and hold for \emph{any} static description of the fluid, elastic or
reduced.

\textbf{Step 3 (the displacement description adds kinematics).} The
elastic fluid also carries a displacement, and \eqref{eq:constitutive}
gives its dilatation in two independent ways.

\emph{Mechanically}, from the Lagrangian pressure. By
\eqref{eq:constitutive} and \eqref{eq:p-slaved},
\begin{equation}
  \Delta p = p^1 - \rho g\,u_r = -\rho\,\phi - \rho g\,u_r
  = -\rho\,(\phi + g\,u_r) ,
  \label{eq:dp-slaved}
\end{equation}
and $\Div\bu = -\Delta p/\kappa$, so
\begin{equation}
  \Div\bu = \frac{\rho}{\kappa}\,(\phi + g\,u_r)
  = \frac{\rho g}{\kappa}\Bigl(u_r + \frac{\phi}{g}\Bigr) .
  \label{eq:div-mech}
\end{equation}

\emph{Kinematically}, from mass conservation. By
\eqref{eq:constitutive}, $\rho^1 = -\rho\,\Div\bu - \rho'\,u_r$, so
$\Div\bu = -(\rho^1 + \rho'\,u_r)/\rho$, and with
\eqref{eq:rho-slaved},
\begin{equation}
  \Div\bu = -\frac{1}{\rho}\Bigl(\frac{\rho'}{g}\,\phi + \rho'\,u_r\Bigr)
  = -\frac{\rho'}{\rho}\Bigl(u_r + \frac{\phi}{g}\Bigr) .
  \label{eq:div-kin}
\end{equation}

Both hold in the elastic fluid, so their right-hand sides are equal:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\rho g}{\kappa}\Bigl(u_r + \frac{\phi}{g}\Bigr)
  = -\frac{\rho'}{\rho}\Bigl(u_r + \frac{\phi}{g}\Bigr) .
\end{equation}
Bring both terms to the left and collect the $u_r$ and $\phi$ terms:
\begin{equation}
  u_r\,\Bigl(\frac{\rho g}{\kappa} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}\Bigr)
  = -\frac{\phi}{g}\,
    \Bigl(\frac{\rho g}{\kappa} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}\Bigr) .
\end{equation}
By the definition \eqref{eq:N2} of the buoyancy frequency the bracket
is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\rho g}{\kappa} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}r}
  = -\frac{N^2}{g} ,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  -\frac{N^2}{g}\,u_r = \frac{N^2}{g^2}\,\phi ,
\end{equation}
and multiplying by $-\rho$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;u_r\,\frac{\rho N^2}{g}
   \;=\; -\,\frac{\rho N^2}{g^2}\,\phi\;}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  u_r = -\frac{\phi}{g}
  \quad\text{whenever } N^2 \neq 0 .
  \label{eq:slaving}
\end{equation}
The radial displacement is \emph{slaved}, pointwise, to the potential
perturbation. In words: the Lagrangian perturbation of the
gravitational potential, the change of $\Phi_0 + \phi$ following a
fluid parcel,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Phi = \phi + \bu\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = \phi + g\,u_r ,
  \label{eq:dPhi}
\end{equation}
vanishes: \eqref{eq:slaving} is $\Delta\Phi = 0$, and fluid parcels ride
the perturbed equipotentials. In this notation \eqref{eq:dp-slaved} and
\eqref{eq:div-mech} read $\Delta p = -\rho\,\Delta\Phi$ and
$\Div\bu = (\rho/\kappa)\,\Delta\Phi$, so on the slaved field the
Lagrangian pressure perturbation and the dilatation vanish too, and
$\rho^1 = -\rho'\,u_r$ is the density change of parcels moved radially
without compression --- consistent with \eqref{eq:rho-slaved}.

At $N^2 = 0$ the relation degenerates to $0 = 0$, and the two
determinations of the dilatation show why. The neutral profile has
$\rho'/\rho = -\rho g/\kappa$, so the right-hand sides of
\eqref{eq:div-mech} and \eqref{eq:div-kin} coincide identically, for
every $u_r$: the kinematic and the mechanical determinations of
$\Div\bu$ are one equation, not two, and $u_r$ is free --- the first
appearance of the dichotomy that organises everything below.

\textbf{Step 4 (the interface).} Steps 1--3 hold at every point of the
fluid, up to $\Sigma$. The potential perturbation and the gravity are
continuous across $\Sigma$ ($\phi$ because the potential is, $g$
because the enclosed mass is), so where $N^2 \neq 0$ on the fluid side
of the interface, \eqref{eq:slaving} fixes the fluid's trace of the
radial displacement:
\begin{equation}
  u_r(\text{fluid side of }\Sigma) = -\frac{\phi(c)}{g(c)} .
  \label{eq:fluid-trace}
\end{equation}
Every formulation that carries a fluid displacement also imposes a
matching condition at $\Sigma$. Welded: the whole displacement is
continuous, $\jump{\bu} = \mathbf 0$. Slipping: only its normal
component is, $\bn\cdot\jump{\bu} = 0$. Both contain
\begin{equation}
  u_r(\text{fluid side of }\Sigma) = u_r(\text{solid side of }\Sigma).
  \label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
Together, \eqref{eq:fluid-trace} and \eqref{eq:matching} require
$u_r(\text{solid side of }\Sigma) = -\phi(c)/g(c)$, that is
$\Delta\Phi = 0$ on the solid side: the solid's boundary would have to
ride the perturbed equipotential. Nothing else in the problem asks for
this. Steps 1 and 2 together with the solid already make a closed and
well-posed problem --- Dahlen's (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}) --- which
determines the solid's response, and with it $u_r$ on the solid side
of $\Sigma$ and $\phi(c)$; for a generic load that $u_r$ is not
$-\phi(c)/g(c)$, and the interior leaves no freedom to absorb the
difference, since \eqref{eq:slaving} fixes $u_r$ pointwise. One trace
is asked to take two values.

Dahlen's closure keeps only Steps 1 and 2, never introduces a
displacement in the fluid, and so never meets Steps 3 and 4: the mass
that the slaved field would sweep across $\Sigma$ it books as the
surface term F3 (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}).

\section{Non-existence of the elastic-static solution}
\label{sec:nonexist}

\emph{Status: argued, and displayed numerically.} Step 4 of
\S\ref{sec:slaving} is the whole obstruction: the interior slaving
\eqref{eq:slaving} fixes the fluid's trace \eqref{eq:fluid-trace}, the
matching \eqref{eq:matching} demands that the same trace equal the
solid's, and for a generic load the two values differ. Hence:

\begin{claim}[no classical static solution]
\label{claim:nonexist}
For $N^2 \ne 0$ and generic loading, the static problem of the
elastic fluid --- welded or slipping alike, since both impose
\eqref{eq:matching} --- has no solution in any function space in
which the fluid displacement has a normal trace at $\Sigma$
(classical, or $H^1$-conforming weak). The over-determination sits at
the interface; no interior rearrangement relieves it.
\end{claim}

Three remarks on what this does and does not say.

\begin{remark}[the variational face]
In weak form the pathology appears as spectrum, not as a visible
contradiction: the stratification contributes a continuum of
near-neutral fluid motions whose energies accumulate at zero
(for the dynamic operator, the essential spectrum is an interval with
endpoint $0$ whenever $N^2 \neq 0$ somewhere). Zero is then not an
isolated eigenvalue with a clean kernel but an accumulation point;
the static operator's range is not closed; and a generic load has a
component outside it. For stable stratification the energy picture is
loss of compactness: minimising sequences push the violation of
\eqref{eq:slaving} into ever-thinner structure at $\Sigma$, the
infimum is approached but not attained, and the limit object leaves
the space. For $N^2 < 0$ the same occurs in saddle form. Turning
this paragraph into a theorem (non-closed range; characterisation of
the compatible loads) is open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what computations produce instead]
A discretised and/or regularised problem is finite-dimensional and
always has an answer. But it approximates an escaping sequence, not a
solution: the computed fluid displacement never settles (it depends
on the mesh and on the regularisation parameter at fixed accuracy),
while gauge-invariant solid observables hover in a band whose size is
controlled by the stratification. The measured bands are in
\S\ref{sec:evidence}. We emphasise: this is not a solver deficiency,
and no amount of resolution removes it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[there is no boundary layer to resolve]
One might expect the solution to consist of the slaved interior plus
a thin matching layer at $\Sigma$. Numerically no such layer appears
at any regularisation we tried: statics contains no mechanism to set
a layer width (no viscosity, no diffusion), so the would-be layer has
zero width --- which is just Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist} again. The
layer becomes real, with a physical width, only when dissipation is
restored (\S\ref{sec:regular}).
\end{remark}

\section{The gauge-theoretic statement}
\label{sec:gauge}

\emph{Status: derived (the invariance observation); argued (the
dichotomy).} Two observations place the obstruction relative to the
FRL symmetry.

First, every relabelling of either class satisfies
$\bw\cdot\bn = 0$ on $\Sigma$, so no gauge transformation alters the
normal trace: the incompatibility of
\eqref{eq:slaving}--\eqref{eq:matching} is untouched by any choice of
representative. Second, and more sharply: for $N^2 \ne 0$ the strict
class is tangent to the level surfaces, so its members have
$w_r = 0$ \emph{pointwise} --- the radial displacement of the fluid
is gauge-\emph{invariant} precisely when \eqref{eq:slaving} pins it.
At $N^2 = 0$, where class K makes $u_r$ gauge-variable, the pinning
equation is the one that degenerates. The geometry of the symmetry
group and the solvability of the field equations switch together.

\begin{proposition}[well-posedness on the quotient]
\label{prop:quotient}
The static problem is well posed on the quotient by the fluid
relabellings if and only if $N^2 = 0$. At $N^2 = 0$ the zero modes of
the static operator form a genuine null space (class K), every load
is compatible with it (relabellings are energy- and load-neutral),
and the solution set is a single nonempty orbit; the
Maitra--Al-Attar selection --- normal continuity, free slip, a
representative picked by minimising a gauge functional --- is then a
well-posed choice among existing solutions. For $N^2 \ne 0$ the
degeneracy at zero is essential spectrum rather than kernel, the
solution set is empty for generic loads, and the selection step has
nothing to select from: the gauge-fixed discrete answer it returns is
the regularised object of the previous section, with its band.
\emph{(The ``only if'' direction is Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist}; the
``if'' direction at $N^2=0$ is the class-K lifting argument of the
removability analysis, and is confirmed numerically to $10^{-10}$.)}
\end{proposition}

This is, we believe, the clean way to say what is wrong and what is
right simultaneously: nothing in the relabelling programme is at
fault --- it is exactly the framework in which the dichotomy
kernel-versus-essential-spectrum can be stated --- but its selection
step inherits the solvability of the quotient problem, which is a
property of the stratification, not of the method.

\section{Dahlen's method as the secular closure}
\label{sec:dahlen}

\emph{Status: derived (the identification); measured (the
$\kappa$-blindness and the null test).} Dahlen's elimination keeps
only \eqref{eq:p-slaved}--\eqref{eq:rho-slaved} --- which are exact
--- and never introduces a fluid displacement, so
Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist} cannot touch it: its static problem is
well posed (and, in our one-dimensional reference, converges to
machine precision where the elastic-static solves wander). What
happened to the conflict? Reconstruct the displacement implied by
Dahlen's fields through $\rho^1 = -\Div(\rho\bu)$: it is the slaved
field $u_r = -\phi/g$, which does \emph{not} match the solid at
$\Sigma$. The formalism remains consistent because its interface
term
\[
  -\int_{\Sigma} \rho_F\,
     \bigl[\phi\,(\mathbf{m}\cdot\bu') + \phi'\,(\mathbf{m}\cdot\bu)\bigr]\dS
\]
(the ``F3'' term of the implementation; $\mathbf{m}$ the solid's
outward normal, $\rho_F$ the fluid-side density) carries the mass
displaced \emph{across} the boundary as a surface density. In the
displacement picture this is a normal-displacement discontinuity at
the CMB: the fluid slips normally past the boundary and the swept
mass is booked on $\Sigma$. That is the \emph{secular closure}: the
state reached after the fluid has rearranged to hydrostatic
equilibrium in the perturbed potential, however long that takes.

Two sharp consequences, both verified:
(i) at $\ell \ge 1$ the construction never consults the fluid's bulk
modulus, so Dahlen's answer is exactly independent of $\kappa$ in the
core (measured: unchanged to $10^{-11}$ under a thirty-fold scaling);
(ii) at $N^2 = 0$ the reduction coincides with the full elastic
static fluid, so Dahlen, welded and slip must agree exactly there
(measured: $\sim 10^{-10}$ at every degree on \code{aw\_core}). The
well-known failure at $\ell = 0$ is a separate, understood matter:
there the tangential balance is empty (Step 1 of
\S\ref{sec:slaving}), so $p^1 + \rho\,\phi$ is a free function of
radius in each fluid region, and the compressional equations --- which
the elimination discards --- are what would determine it
(\code{doc/benchmark\_programme.tex}, ``Degree zero with a fluid
region''). The practical remedy (solve $\ell = 0$ with the displacement description) is what
this library and \code{pyslfp} both do.

\paragraph{Aspherical reference states.} The elimination above is a
spherical accident. Its first step --- tangential balance integrating
to $p^1 = -\rho\,\phi$ degree by degree --- used the fact that on a
spherical background the level surfaces are the coordinate spheres
and the harmonics decouple. On a properly aspherical hydrostatic
reference the same balance says only that $p^1 + \rho\,\phi$ is
constant on each level surface of $\Phi_0$: the elimination leaves a
free \emph{function} of $\Phi_0$ per connected fluid region,
coupled to every harmonic through Poisson's equation
(\code{doc/quasi\_static\_models.tex}, assumption M4). The secular
\emph{state} still exists on any geometry --- the fluid rearranged
to hydrostatic equilibrium in the perturbed potential is a
well-defined configuration --- but Dahlen's \emph{route} to it does
not survive asphericity in any practically implementable form. Since
the full-elastic static formulations are the ill-posed ones at
$N^2 \ne 0$, an aspherical model with a non-neutral core currently
has \emph{no} practical well-posed static method at all; the
dissipative route of \S\ref{sec:regular} is then not a consistency
check but the only general-geometry computation of the secular
response on offer. For this library's aspherical programme
(equilibrium figures, aspherical reference states) the point is
practical, not academic.

\begin{remark}[a historical irony]
Dahlen criticised, forcefully, a 1970s proposal of Crossley and
Gubbins that the static deformation problem admits a discontinuity of
radial displacement at the CMB. The analysis above says that his own
static method carries exactly such a discontinuity, implicitly, as
the surface-mass bookkeeping of the secular closure --- and that for
$N^2 \ne 0$ some such discontinuity is \emph{necessary}, since no
static solution without one exists. The only question a formulation
answers is whether it admits the jump openly and is well posed, or
forbids it and loses existence.
\end{remark}

\section[Generalisation: hydrostatic fluid regions in a three-dimensional model]{Generalisation: hydrostatic fluid regions\newline
in a three-dimensional model}
\label{sec:general}

\emph{Status: a sketch under review. Each step is marked
\emph{derived}, \emph{argued} or \emph{conjectured}; where the sketch
as first proposed did not survive the derivation, the text says so.}
The question is whether the secular closure of \S\ref{sec:dahlen} ---
Steps 1 and 2 of \S\ref{sec:slaving}, the fluid displacement never
introduced --- extends beyond the spherical background, and whether the
same analysis decides degree 0 (the free function of Step 1).

\paragraph{Setting.} A fluid region $F$ (inviscid, barotropic-elastic
with $V(\mathbf x, J)$ as in \S\ref{sec:setting}) is in hydrostatic
equilibrium in the total background potential $\Phi_0$ of a body whose
solid parts are arbitrary: aspherical, pre-stressed, non-hydrostatic.
Hydrostatic means $\nabla p^0 = -\rho\nabla\Phi_0$ in $F$. Then
$\nabla p^0 \times \nabla\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0$, and, taking the curl,
$\nabla\rho \times \nabla\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0$: $p^0$ and $\rho$ are
constant on each connected component of each level set of $\Phi_0$ in
$F$, and $F$ carries the level foliation of $\Phi_0$. (They are single
functions of $\Phi_0$ across the whole of $F$ only if the material is
layered on the level surfaces; we write $\rho(\Phi_0)$ and
$\rho_\Phi = \mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}\Phi_0$ per component.) Write
$g = |\nabla\Phi_0| > 0$ in $F$ (away from a centre) and
$\nuh = \nabla\Phi_0/g$ for the unit normal to the level surfaces,
pointing towards increasing $\Phi_0$; $\nabla_{\!\parallel}$ is the
part of the gradient along the level surfaces. The fluid--solid
interface $\Sigma$, with unit normal $\bn$ pointing out of $F$, is
\emph{not} assumed to be a level surface: an enclosed fluid is in
equilibrium against a wall of any shape, the solid supplying the
traction (only a free surface must be level). Let $\theta$ be the angle
between $\Sigma$ and the level surface through a point of $\Sigma$, so
that $\bn = \cos\theta\,\nuh + \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf t}$ with
$\hat{\mathbf t}$ a unit vector along the level surface, normal to the
curve $\Sigma \cap \{\Phi_0 = \mathrm{const}\}$; $\theta = 0$ on a
level wall, the spherical case. The buoyancy frequency generalises to
\begin{equation}
  N^2 = -g^2\Bigl(\frac{\rho_\Phi}{\rho} + \frac{\rho}{\kappa}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:N2-general}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:N2} on the sphere, where $\rho' = g\rho_\Phi$. The
conventions of \S\ref{sec:slaving} are kept: the static momentum
balance \eqref{eq:momentum}, the relations \eqref{eq:constitutive},
and $\nabla^2\phi = 4\pi G\rho^1$.

\paragraph{Step G1 (interior relations). \emph{Derived.}} The
along-level part of \eqref{eq:momentum} has no $\rho^1$-term, since
$\nabla_{\!\parallel}\Phi_0 = \mathbf 0$, and $\rho$ is constant on the
level component, so as in Step 1 of \S\ref{sec:slaving}
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\!\parallel}\bigl(p^1 + \rho\,\phi\bigr) = \mathbf 0,
  \qquad
  p^1 + \rho\,\phi = f(\Phi_0),
  \label{eq:G-f}
\end{equation}
with $f$ a free function, one value for each connected component of
each level set in $F$. With a non-level wall the level sets within $F$
are cut by $\Sigma$ and may be disconnected; each component then
carries its own value of $f$, and $f$ must join continuously where
components merge, since $p^1$ is continuous in $F$. The normal part of
\eqref{eq:momentum}, with $\nuh\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = g$, is
$-\partial_\nu p^1 - \rho\,\partial_\nu\phi - \rho^1 g = 0$. From
\eqref{eq:G-f}, $\partial_\nu p^1 = -\rho_\Phi g\,\phi - \rho\,\partial_\nu\phi
+ f' g$ (since $\partial_\nu\rho = \rho_\Phi g$ and
$\partial_\nu f = f' g$, primes now $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\Phi_0$), so
\begin{equation}
  \rho_\Phi g\,\phi + \rho\,\partial_\nu\phi - f' g - \rho\,\partial_\nu\phi
  - \rho^1 g = 0,
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  \rho^1 = \rho_\Phi\,\phi - f'(\Phi_0) .
  \label{eq:G-rho}
\end{equation}
These generalise Steps 1--2 of \S\ref{sec:slaving}: on the sphere at
$\ell \ge 1$, $f = 0$ and \eqref{eq:G-rho} is \eqref{eq:rho-slaved}.
Dahlen's reduction is \eqref{eq:G-f}--\eqref{eq:G-rho} with nothing
more; it leaves $f$ free.

\paragraph{Step G2 (kinematics with $f \neq 0$). \emph{Derived.}}
Define $s$ by
\begin{equation}
  \bu\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = -\phi - s,
  \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
  s = -(\phi + \bu\cdot\nabla\Phi_0) = -\Delta\Phi ,
  \label{eq:G-s}
\end{equation}
minus the Lagrangian perturbation \eqref{eq:dPhi} of the potential. With
$\bu\cdot\nabla p^0 = -\rho\,\bu\cdot\nabla\Phi_0 = \rho(\phi + s)$ and
\eqref{eq:G-f},
\begin{equation}
  \Delta p = p^1 + \bu\cdot\nabla p^0 = -\rho\phi + f + \rho\phi + \rho s
  = f + \rho s ,
  \qquad
  \Div\bu = -\frac{\Delta p}{\kappa} = -\frac{f + \rho s}{\kappa} .
  \label{eq:G-div-mech}
\end{equation}
Mass conservation, with $\nabla\rho = \rho_\Phi\nabla\Phi_0$, gives
$\rho^1 = -\rho\,\Div\bu - \rho_\Phi\,\bu\cdot\nabla\Phi_0
= -\rho\,\Div\bu + \rho_\Phi(\phi + s)$. Equating with
\eqref{eq:G-rho}, the $\rho_\Phi\phi$ terms cancel:
\begin{equation}
  -f' = -\rho\,\Div\bu + \rho_\Phi\, s,
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad
  \rho\,\Div\bu = f' + \rho_\Phi\, s .
  \label{eq:G-div-kin}
\end{equation}
Insert \eqref{eq:G-div-mech}:
$-\rho(f + \rho s)/\kappa = f' + \rho_\Phi s$, that is
\begin{equation}
  -s\Bigl(\rho_\Phi + \frac{\rho^2}{\kappa}\Bigr) = f' + \frac{\rho f}{\kappa} .
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:N2-general}, $\rho_\Phi + \rho^2/\kappa = -\rho N^2/g^2$, so
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;s\,\frac{\rho N^2}{g^2} = f' + \frac{\rho f}{\kappa}\;}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  s = \frac{g^2}{\rho N^2}\Bigl(f' + \frac{\rho f}{\kappa}\Bigr)
  \quad (N^2 \neq 0),
  \qquad
  \Div\bu = -\frac{f + \rho s}{\kappa} .
  \label{eq:G-slaving}
\end{equation}
The component of $\bu$ along $\nabla\Phi_0$ and the dilatation are
slaved, pointwise, to $f$ and $f'$; the interior does not determine
$f$ itself. With $f = 0$, $s = 0$, which is \eqref{eq:slaving}. Two
remarks. First, if $\kappa$ is constant on the level surfaces (true on
the spherical ladder, and on any body whose material is layered on its
level surfaces), then $N^2/g^2$ and $s$ are functions of $\Phi_0$ alone:
every material surface that was a level surface $\Phi_0 = c$ ends on
the level surface of the perturbed potential $\Phi_0 + \phi$ with value
$c - s(c)$. Parcel surfaces remain level surfaces, which in the sketch
was an assumption about stable ordering; here it is a consequence of
\eqref{eq:G-slaving}. Second, at $N^2 = 0$ the boxed relation reads
$0 = f' + \rho f/\kappa$: the interior then fixes $f$ up to one
constant per region ($f \propto \exp(-\int\rho/\kappa\,\mathrm d\Phi_0)$)
and leaves $s$ free, the class-K gauge freedom of \S\ref{sec:gauge}.

\emph{On the documented degree-0 statements.} The sketch proposed that
at $\ell = 0$ on the sphere $f = f_0$ is a constant, pinned by the
degree-0 matching. That is not right for $N^2 \neq 0$. Take a uniform
core ($\rho_\Phi = 0$, so $N^2 = -g^2\rho/\kappa$) and $f = f_0$. Then
\eqref{eq:G-slaving} gives $s = -f_0/\rho$ and $\Div\bu = 0$;
\eqref{eq:G-rho} gives $\rho^1 = 0$, so $\phi$ is harmonic, and at
$\ell = 0$ regular, $\phi = \phi_0$; hence
$u_r = -(\phi_0 + s)/g \propto 1/r$ (as $g \propto r$), whose
divergence $r^{-2}(r^2u_r)'$ is nonzero unless $u_r \equiv 0$. A
constant $f$ therefore forces $u_r = 0$ throughout the core, which a
generic degree-0 load cannot match. What is true is the count. Dahlen's
reduction leaves $f$ a free function. The displacement description adds,
at $\ell = 0$, the identity $\Div\bu = r^{-2}(r^2 u_r)'$; with
\eqref{eq:G-slaving} this is a second-order equation for $f$ (Step G5),
whose regular solutions form a one-parameter family, the parameter
pinned by one wall condition. ``One constant per fluid region'' is thus
the right count after the interior kinematics; ``a free function'' is
the right count for Dahlen's reduction alone; and at $N^2 = 0$ the
constant appears directly, as above. The two statements in the
documentation (\S\ref{sec:dahlen} and \code{doc/benchmark\_programme.tex},
``Degree zero with a fluid region'') are reconciled in this sense, not
by $f$ being constant.

\paragraph{Step G3 (the relabelling class on a non-level wall).
\emph{Derived.}} A relabelling of the strict class ($N^2 \neq 0$) is
divergence-free and tangent to the level surfaces, $\bw\cdot\nabla\Phi_0
= 0$, and must not move the boundary, $\bw\cdot\bn = 0$ on $\Sigma$. On
a level wall $\bn = \nuh$, the two conditions coincide, and every
along-level motion at the wall is admissible: the strict class of
\S\ref{sec:setting}. On a transverse wall ($\sin\theta \neq 0$) a field
tangent to both the level surface and $\Sigma$ lies along the curve
$\Sigma\cap\{\Phi_0 = c\}$; the admissible class at the wall reduces to
motions along those curves (in two dimensions, where the curves are
points, to no motion at all). An along-level displacement $a\,\hat{\mathbf t}$
at the wall has $\bn$-component $a\sin\theta$ and carries mass across
$\Sigma$. Consequence: the statement ``tangential slip is pure gauge''
holds only on level walls. On a transverse wall a slip tangent to
$\Sigma$ has a component along $\nabla\Phi_0$ (of size $\sin\theta$)
except along the intersection curves, and so is not a relabelling.

\paragraph{Step G4 (matching on a transverse wall). \emph{Necessity
derived; sufficiency argued; the level limit and F3 conjectured.}}
Decompose the fluid displacement as $\bu = u_\nu\nuh + \bu_\parallel$,
with $u_\nu = -(\phi + s)/g$ slaved by \eqref{eq:G-s}. The normal
matching imposed by every displacement formulation,
$\bu\cdot\bn = \bu_s\cdot\bn$ on $\Sigma$ ($\bu_s$ the solid's
displacement), reads
\begin{equation}
  u_\nu\cos\theta + (\bu_\parallel\cdot\hat{\mathbf t})\sin\theta
  = \bu_s\cdot\bn ,
  \qquad
  \bu_\parallel\cdot\hat{\mathbf t}
  = \frac{\bu_s\cdot\bn - u_\nu\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} .
  \label{eq:G-match}
\end{equation}
It involves the along-level component, which the interior does not fix
pointwise: \eqref{eq:G-slaving} fixes $\Div\bu$, hence only the
divergence of $\bu_\parallel$ on each level surface. (For a field
$\mathbf v$ tangent to the level surfaces,
$\Div\mathbf v = g\,\Div_{\parallel}(\mathbf v/g)$, because
$\nuh\cdot\partial_\nu\mathbf v = -\mathbf v\cdot\partial_\nu\nuh$ and
$\partial_\nu\nuh = \nabla_{\!\parallel}\ln g$.) On each component
$L_c$ of a level set, $\bu_\parallel/g$ therefore has prescribed surface
divergence and, by \eqref{eq:G-match}, prescribed conormal flux on the
curve $\partial L_c \subset \Sigma$. That Neumann-type problem on $L_c$
is solvable if and only if the two are compatible, \emph{one scalar
condition per component}; the remaining freedom is the strict
relabelling class. The compatibility is the integral of mass
conservation over $R_c = \{\Phi_0 < c\}\cap F$ (the component below
$L_c$), whose boundary is $L_c$ (outward normal $\nuh$) and the wall
patch $\Sigma_c = \Sigma\cap\{\Phi_0 < c\}$ (outward normal $\bn$):
\begin{equation}
  \delta M_\Sigma(c) \;=\; \int_{R_c}\rho^1\dV
  \;-\; \int_{L_c}\frac{\rho\,(\phi + s)}{g}\dS
  \;+\; \int_{\Sigma_c}\rho\,\bu_s\cdot\bn\dS \;=\; 0 ,
  \label{eq:G-band}
\end{equation}
using $\int_{R_c}\rho^1 = -\oint_{\partial R_c}\rho\,\bu\cdot\bn_{\text{out}}$,
$\bu\cdot\nuh = u_\nu = -(\phi+s)/g$ on $L_c$, and the matching on
$\Sigma_c$. Its derivative in $c$ is the per-band balance of the sketch:
the compressional change of the band (through $f$, via $\rho^1$ and
$s$) against the flux through its wall patch. So on a transverse wall
the matching can be met pointwise, provided \eqref{eq:G-band} holds for
every $c$, and \eqref{eq:G-band} is one condition per level component,
the same count as $f$. The over-determination of
Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist} does not arise there; it is a property of
level walls. Two qualifications, both open. First, by
\eqref{eq:G-match} the along-level displacement scales as
$1/\sin\theta$ and diverges as the wall becomes level, and every closed
wall is level somewhere: at the extrema of $\Phi_0$ restricted to
$\Sigma$, $\nabla\Phi_0$ is normal to $\Sigma$ and $\theta = 0$. Whether
the displacement field near those points has finite energy is not
settled. Second, only the normal matching (slip) is analysed; the
welded case imposes the tangential components as well, and its count is
not done.

\emph{The level limit (conjectured).} As $\theta \to 0$ on an open set
(a wall becoming level), the range of levels cut by the wall collapses
to the wall's level, the along-level displacement of size $1/\theta$
lives in a layer of thickness of order $\theta$, and the along-level
volume transport it carries stays finite: in the limit, a transport
along the wall concentrated on $\Sigma$, whose divergence is a surface
density of zero total (it only redistributes mass along the wall). The
sphere is thus the singular limit of the general problem, and Dahlen's
surface-mass bookkeeping is what survives of the transverse matching.
That this limiting surface density is exactly what the F3 term of
\S\ref{sec:dahlen} books is \emph{conjectured}, not derived: F3 as
written books $\rho_F$ times the solid's normal displacement against
$\phi$, the Eulerian effect of the wall sweeping fluid, and identifying
it with the collapsed layer needs the limit of the weak forms, which is
not done here.

\paragraph{Step G5 (the level-wall condition and its equation for $f$).
\emph{Derived, under the stated assumptions; uniqueness conjectured.}}
On a level wall the level sets in $F$ are closed surfaces $L_c$,
$c_{\min} < c < c_w$, with $\Sigma = L_{c_w}$, and \eqref{eq:G-band}
has no wall term below the wall:
\begin{equation}
  \delta M(c) = \int_{\Phi_0 < c}\rho^1\dV
  - \oint_{L_c}\frac{\rho\,(\phi + s)}{g}\dS = 0,
  \qquad c_{\min} < c < c_w .
  \label{eq:G-dM}
\end{equation}
This is exact kinematics --- mass conservation of the region below each
level surface, given the slaved normal displacement --- and needs no
argument about the ordering of parcels. Assume $\kappa$ constant on the
level surfaces, so that $\rho$, $\rho_\Phi$, $\kappa$, $N^2/g^2$, $f$
and $s$ are functions of $c$, and define
\begin{equation}
  A(c) = \oint_{L_c}\frac{\mathrm{d}S}{g},
  \qquad
  \bar\Phi(c) = \oint_{L_c}\frac{\phi}{g}\dS,
  \qquad
  D(c) = \rho_\Phi + \frac{\rho^2}{\kappa} = -\frac{\rho N^2}{g^2} ,
\end{equation}
so that $A$ is the volume per unit potential and, by the coarea
formula, $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dc}\int_{\Phi_0<c}F\dV =
\oint_{L_c}F\,\mathrm dS/g$. With \eqref{eq:G-rho},
\begin{equation}
  \delta M'(c) = \rho_\Phi\bar\Phi - f'A
  - \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dc}\bigl[\rho\bar\Phi + \rho\,s\,A\bigr]
  = -f'A - \rho\,\bar\Phi' - (\rho A s)' ,
\end{equation}
so $\delta M' = 0$ is $(\rho A s)' + A f' = -\rho\bar\Phi'$. Together
with the boxed relation of \eqref{eq:G-slaving}, written
$f' + \rho f/\kappa = -D s$, this is the first-order system
\begin{equation}
  f' = -D\,s - \frac{\rho}{\kappa}\,f ,
  \qquad
  (\rho A s)' = A\Bigl(D\,s + \frac{\rho}{\kappa}\,f\Bigr) - \rho\,\bar\Phi' ,
  \label{eq:G-system}
\end{equation}
driven by $\bar\Phi'$, the $c$-derivative of the level-surface integral
of $\phi$. Where $N^2 \neq 0$, $s$ can be eliminated to give the
second-order equation of the sketch,
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl[\frac{\rho A}{D}\Bigl(f' + \frac{\rho f}{\kappa}\Bigr)\Bigr]'
  - A\,f' = \rho\,\bar\Phi' .
  \label{eq:G-ode}
\end{equation}
The form \eqref{eq:G-system} is preferable: \eqref{eq:G-ode} is singular
wherever $N^2$ passes through zero inside $F$ (\code{stratified\_core}
does, near 1670\,km), while \eqref{eq:G-system} is regular there (at
$D = 0$ the two equations merely decouple). The conditions are two.
(i) Regularity at $c_{\min}$ (the centre, where $A \to 0$), which also
gives $\delta M(c_{\min}) = 0$ and so, with $\delta M' = 0$,
\eqref{eq:G-dM} at every level. (ii) At the wall, \emph{zero net
surface mass}. The sketch proposed conservation of the region's total
mass including surface mass; that is vacuous. With the surface density
$\sigma = \rho_F(u_\nu - \bu_s\cdot\bn)$ (the slaved fluid's normal
displacement against the wall's), the container's total mass, volume
plus surface, is $\int_F\rho^1 + \oint\rho_F\,u_\nu$, which vanishes by
\eqref{eq:G-dM} at $c_w$ for every member of the regular family, so it
fixes nothing. The physical condition is
that the surface layer only redistributes mass along the wall, as the
level limit of Step G4 gives (the closed bands below the wall conserve
their own mass, so none enters the layer): $\oint_\Sigma\sigma\dS = 0$,
that is,
\begin{equation}
  s(c_w)\,A(c_w) = -\bar\Phi(c_w) - \oint_\Sigma \bu_s\cdot\bn\dS ,
  \label{eq:G-wall}
\end{equation}
equivalently $\int_F\rho^1\dV + \oint_\Sigma\rho_F\,\bu_s\cdot\bn\dS = 0$:
the fluid mass inside the displaced container, surface mass excluded,
is conserved. This wall condition is \emph{argued}; the rest of the
step is derived.

\emph{On the sphere (derived).} $A = 4\pi r^2/g$,
$\mathrm d/\mathrm dc = g^{-1}\mathrm d/\mathrm dr$, and $\bar\Phi$
sees only the degree-0 part of $\phi$. For a load of degree
$\ell \ge 1$, $\bar\Phi = 0$ and $\oint_\Sigma\bu_s\cdot\bn = 0$, so
\eqref{eq:G-system}--\eqref{eq:G-wall} are homogeneous and $f = s = 0$
solves them (that it is the only solution is the uniqueness question
below), recovering \eqref{eq:p-slaved}--\eqref{eq:slaving}. At
$\ell = 0$: with $s = -(\phi + g u_r)$, $A s = -\bar\Phi - 4\pi r^2 u_r$.
Substitute into the second equation of \eqref{eq:G-system}, write
$D s + \rho f/\kappa = \rho_\Phi s + (\rho/\kappa)(f + \rho s)
= \rho_\Phi s - \rho\,\Div\bu$ by \eqref{eq:G-div-mech}, and expand
$(\rho A s)' = -\rho_\Phi\bar\Phi - \rho\bar\Phi' - 4\pi\rho_\Phi r^2 u_r
- \rho\,(4\pi r^2 u_r)'$. The terms in $\bar\Phi'$ and in $\rho_\Phi$
cancel between the two sides, leaving
$\rho\,(4\pi r^2 u_r)' = \rho A\,\Div\bu$ (primes $\mathrm d/\mathrm d\Phi_0
= g^{-1}\mathrm d/\mathrm dr$), that is $\Div\bu = r^{-2}(r^2 u_r)'$ with
the radial derivative: the degree-0 kinematic identity. The system is the spectral degree-0
problem (full compressible equations, fluid as a $\mu = 0$ solid)
rewritten in $(f, s)$, and the wall condition \eqref{eq:G-wall} is, for
a uniform $\sigma$, $\sigma = 0$: continuity of $u_r$, the degree-0
matching.

\emph{Transverse to level (argued).} On a transverse wall,
\eqref{eq:G-band} below the wall's lowest level is \eqref{eq:G-dM};
within the cut range it carries the wall flux; at the wall's highest
level ($R_c = F$, $L_c$ empty) it is the container's mass balance
$\int_F\rho^1 + \oint\rho_F\,\bu_s\cdot\bn = 0$. As $\theta \to 0$ the
cut range collapses to $c_w$, and the condition just below with the
condition at the top gives \eqref{eq:G-dM} at every level and
\eqref{eq:G-wall}. The per-band wall condition thus reduces to the
level-wall problem in the limit, but the limit is not made rigorous.

\emph{Not derived.} Uniqueness of \eqref{eq:G-system}--\eqref{eq:G-wall}
(the coupling to $\phi$ through Poisson's equation, $\rho^1 = \rho_\Phi\phi
- f'$, and to the solid through the wall pressure is part of it); its
character for $N^2 < 0$, where $D$ changes sign; the case of $\kappa$
varying on the level surfaces, where $s$ is not constant on them and
\eqref{eq:G-system} becomes a system with level-surface averages; and
whether the per-band conditions \eqref{eq:G-band} determine $f$ on a
transverse wall (one condition per component, the right count, but no
proof).

\paragraph{Step G6 (the generalised closure as a method).
\emph{Argued; resting on the conjectured parts of G4--G5.}} Eliminate
the fluid displacement. The solid then sees the fluid through: the
volume source $\rho^1 = \rho_\Phi\phi - f'$ in Poisson's equation; the
pressure at the wall's displaced position, $p^1 + \bu_s\cdot\nabla p^0 =
f - \rho_F(\phi + \bu_s\cdot\nabla\Phi_0)$, the interface pressure with
its hydrostatic shift; and, where the wall is level, the surface-mass
term with the slaved normal displacement (Step G4). The function $f$,
with $s$, enters as a one-dimensional unknown per connected component
of level set in each fluid region --- a function of $\Phi_0$, discretised
in one dimension and coupled to the three-dimensional system through
level-surface integrals, bordered in the way the sea-level border carries
its uniform term --- determined by \eqref{eq:G-system}--\eqref{eq:G-wall}
on level walls and by \eqref{eq:G-band} on transverse ones. If the
conjectured parts hold, the result is well posed, needs no relaxation,
gives the secular state on any geometry with hydrostatic fluid regions,
fixes degree 0 on spherical models within the Dahlen framework, and
would supersede the statement of \S\ref{sec:dahlen} (``Aspherical
reference states'') that an aspherical model with a non-neutral core has
no practical well-posed static method. The Maxwell route would remain
the dynamic check.

\emph{What an implementation needs (argued).} Four ingredients, none
of them new machinery. (i) The level foliation of $\Phi_0$ in each
fluid region, from the reference-state solve, with the level-set
integrals $A(c)$ and $\bar\Phi(c)$ evaluated on the mesh (on a
transverse wall also the wall-patch integrals of \eqref{eq:G-band});
on the spherical ladder these are the radial quadratures. (ii) A
one-dimensional discretisation of $(f, s)$ in $c$ on each connected
level component, with the regularity condition at the centre; a few
hundred unknowns at most. (iii) The coupling terms: the volume source
$\rho_\Phi\phi - f'$ in the potential equation, which couples the
three-dimensional $\phi$ to the one-dimensional $f$ and conversely
through $\bar\Phi'$; the wall pressure
$f - \rho_F(\phi + \bu_s\cdot\nabla\Phi_0)$ in the solid's traction;
and the surface-mass term on level walls. These are the F1--F3 terms of
the mixed formulation with $f$ added, so the natural home is a bordered
extension of the Dahlen (mixed) class, the border carrying $(f, s)$ as
the sea-level border carries its uniform term. (iv) The wall condition
\eqref{eq:G-wall} as the border's closing row. On the sphere at
$\ell \ge 1$ the border decouples ($\bar\Phi' = 0$, the right-hand
sides vanish) and the method is Dahlen's; at $\ell = 0$ it is the
spectral degree-0 problem; the test of the construction is therefore
that it reproduces \code{pyslfp} at every degree on the spherical ladder
before any aspherical case is attempted, and the one-dimensional
prototype of open item (vii) is where to start.

\emph{Why it was set aside, and why it is revisited.} This closure was
set aside because it ``dies with the hydrostatic assumption'', and the
Maxwell relaxation was named the general method instead
(\code{doc/benchmark\_programme.tex}, ``Degree zero with a fluid
region'', construction (a)). Two things change the balance. First, only the
\emph{fluid's} hydrostatic state is needed, and an inviscid fluid at
rest has it in any case: the solid parts may be anything. Second, the
implemented Maxwell route reaches the welded limit, not the secular
state, on every non-neutral ladder core, the stable ones included:
degree-1 offsets from \code{pyslfp} of $1.7\times10^{-2}$ on
\code{stable\_core} and $3.4\times10^{-3}$ on \code{stable\_core\_mild}
at $h = 0.3$, order 2, robust to $\beta_0$ and to the preconditioner
settings, every three-dimensional Maxwell solve stopping on the solver
floor (\emph{measured}; reported for this note and not recomputed here).
Two practical requirements. An aspherical $\Sigma$ must come from a
consistent equilibrium figure: on a transverse wall $p^0$ varies along
$\Sigma$, and the solid's pre-stress must balance it. And the Earth's
core--mantle boundary is level to about a part in a thousand, so the
level-wall limit with surface mass is the practically relevant case; the
transverse-wall theory, singular as $\theta \to 0$ by \eqref{eq:G-match},
is needed for general models and would be ill-conditioned near level.

\section{What dissipation selects, and routes to regularisation}
\label{sec:regular}

\emph{Status: argued; the Maxwell route is implemented and measured
(\S\ref{sec:maxwell}).}
Statics is the degenerate limit of some evolution, and the
non-existence of \S\ref{sec:nonexist} is the statement that the
limit depends on physics that statics has discarded. Restoring any of
it gives a well-posed problem:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fluid viscosity.} With viscosity $\eta$, however small,
  the quasi-static evolution under a steady load is well posed; the
  stress supported by the stratification relaxes as the fluid creeps
  along equipotentials, and the $t \to \infty$ state is the
  rearranged, hydrostatic one --- the Dahlen answer. The interface
  region acquires a genuine layer of $\eta$-dependent width that
  thins as $t$ grows.
\item \textbf{Diffusion of the stratifying quantity.} If the density
  anomaly a displaced parcel carries can diffuse (thermally or
  compositionally), the buoyancy constraint itself erodes;
  weak diffusion again selects the rearranged state. This is, as we
  understand it, Wunsch's point from the 1970s: for a stratified
  fluid the static response is not a property of the inviscid
  adiabatic equations at all --- it is selected by the dissipative
  processes, however weak they are.
\item \textbf{A Maxwell viscoelastic core.} Give the core's shear
  response a Maxwell element (artificial shear modulus $\mu_c$,
  relaxation time $\tau$), apply the load as a step, and relax. At
  every Laplace frequency $s > 0$ the moduli are shifted and the
  problem is uniformly well posed, and the $t \to \infty$ limit is
  the secular state. This is the library's production static
  method; \S\ref{sec:maxwell} covers the algorithm, the measured
  trajectory structure (which refines the naive ``relax for a few
  $\tau$'' picture in important ways), the implementation and the
  costs. Its significance is sharpest aspherically
  (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}): where the Dahlen elimination does not
  survive the geometry, the dissipative route is the only practical
  computation of the secular response, and it works on any mesh this
  library can build.
\item \textbf{What does not work.} A static Tikhonov pin (the small
  deviatoric regulariser $\eps_{\mathrm{reg}}$ of the numerical
  tools) makes each solve unique but selects nothing physical: the
  answer depends on $\eps_{\mathrm{reg}}$ with no limit --- measured,
  and expected, since a static regulariser supplies no arrow of time.
  Likewise the frequency-domain route: the response exists for every
  $s \neq 0$ but has no limit as $s \to 0$ (the limit point
  sits on the essential spectrum); the static limit is genuinely
  non-uniform, which is the spectral restatement of the whole
  phenomenon and connects to the classical secular-constant
  (Longman) discussions.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The conclusion inverts the naive reading of
Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist}: the elastic-static problem is not
``the'' static problem that Dahlen approximates; Dahlen's closure is
the static limit that every dissipative completion agrees on, and the
elastic-static formulation is the one asking a question without an
answer. What remains true, and matters, is that the \emph{size} of
the gap between any regularised transient and the secular endpoint is
controlled by the stratification, vanishes identically at
$N^2 = 0$, and is invisible in formulations that never resolve the
closure choice explicitly.

\section{The Maxwell relaxation method}
\label{sec:maxwell}

\emph{Status: implemented (serial and parallel, every problem class),
measured in one, two and three dimensions; the algorithmic facts
below are measured unless marked otherwise.} The idea: since every dissipative completion selects the secular
answer, run one. Make the fluid regions an artificial Maxwell solid,
apply the load as a Heaviside step, and time-step the quasi-static
system by backward Euler until the \emph{solid} region stops moving.
There is then no fluid region in the formulation at all: every step
is a welded solid-elastic solve, the null space is the rigid modes,
and the construction is indifferent to geometry, to the number of
fluid regions, and to their connectivity.

\paragraph{The iteration.} The problem is linear, so the Maxwell
internal variable is the deviatoric fluid strain of a \emph{memory
displacement} $\bw$, and one backward Euler step is
\begin{equation}
  (A + \gamma \hat Q)\,\bu_{n+1} = f + \gamma \hat Q\,\bw_n,
  \qquad
  \bw_{n+1} = \frac{\bw_n + \beta\,\bu_{n+1}}{1+\beta},
  \qquad
  \beta = \frac{\Delta t}{\tau},\quad
  \gamma = \frac{\mu_c}{1+\beta},
  \label{eq:maxwell-step}
\end{equation}
with $A$ the static operator at zero fluid shear and $\hat Q$ the
unit-shear deviatoric fluid stiffness. Three structural facts do all
the work. (i) The fixed point is $\bw = \bu$ with $A\bu = f$: the
relaxed ($\eps \to 0$ welded) solution, \emph{exactly} independent of
$\mu_c$, $\tau$ and the step schedule --- backward Euler's first-order
inaccuracy lives entirely in the transient, which is discarded
(measured: the limit moves by $10^{-13}$ under $\mu_c \times 4$ and
$\Delta t$ halving). (ii) The per-step operator \emph{is} the
gauge-penalty machinery's $A + \eps\mu_c Q$ at
$\eps = 1/(1+\beta)$, and at large \emph{fixed} $\beta$ --- where the
memory update collapses to $w_{n+1} \to u_{n+1}$ ---
\eqref{eq:maxwell-step} is precisely the iterated Tikhonov gauge
refinement of \code{doc/gauged\_fluid.md} at that $\eps$ (the
$\beta \to \infty$ limit itself sends $\eps \to 0$, whereas iterated
Tikhonov holds it fixed) --- so the long-observed
semi-convergence of those refinements on non-neutral cores was the
Rayleigh--Taylor growth below, seen without knowing it. (iii)
Backward Euler is L-stable, so \emph{escalating} $\beta$ damps every
growing mode: large steps turn the integrator into a continuation
solver ($\gamma \to 0$, toward the static welded solve with a
vanishing deviatoric pin). An A-stable, more accurate scheme
(Crank--Nicolson) would ring instead of damping and has no such
endgame.

\paragraph{The measured trajectory.} Three timescales, not two:
an \emph{elastic} phase contracting at $(1+\beta)^{-1}$ per step
($\sim$10 steps); a \emph{configurational} cascade --- viscous
gravitational relaxation of the artificial fluid, rates
$\lambda/\eta$ with $\lambda$ the weak residual stiffness of
near-equipotential motions, measured down to $10^{-4}\mu_c/\eta$, so
physical times $10^4\,\tau$ and beyond (``a few Maxwell times''
completes only the elastic phase); and, at $N^2 < 0$ only, genuine
Rayleigh--Taylor \emph{growth} of the same viscous-flow type. The
ratio of growth to cascade rates is $\mu_c$-independent, of order
$|N^2|L/g$: on the violently non-neutral \code{fluid\_core}
($\approx 300\times$) there is no plateau window at all and the
finite-time plateau is smeared by exactly the ambiguity band
($3.5\,\%$ at the two-dimensional test resolution), while at
$N^2 \ge 0$ there is no growth phase and the relaxation is clean.
The plateau width is a time-domain display of the band; its
degeneracy is the physics, not the method.

\paragraph{The schedule.} The stopping metric is the relative
per-step \emph{solid} displacement increment only --- precisely the
fields the secular limit defines; the fluid displacement does not
converge at $N^2 \ne 0$ and must not be monitored. The stepper runs
a physical phase at $\beta_0$ (default $3$, which resolves the
plateau --- at $N^2 < 0$ larger $\beta_0$ degrades the returned
band-level answer monotonically, measured to the point of
uselessness at $\beta_0 = 100$; at $N^2 \ge 0$, $\beta_0 = 10$--$30$
is $\sim 3\times$ cheaper and converges with no escalation at all),
detects stagnation of the increment, and then escalates $\beta$
geometrically. Three safeguards, each forced by a measured failure:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Best-state checkpointing.} The iterate is a function
  of $(\bw, \eps)$ plus a warm start, so the lowest-increment state
  seen is checkpointed by its memory vector alone and restored by
  one warm re-solve. On any terminal event --- iterative-solver
  conditioning floor, escalation cap, step cap --- the best state is
  returned: when the solver cannot complete the continuation, the
  method degrades \emph{to the physical plateau}, the honest
  band-level answer. (Blind escalation instead injected near-kernel
  noise through the conditioning wall and made the answer worse.)
\item \textbf{Increment-validated inexact stepping.} A fixed-point
  iteration is self-correcting, so each step is solved only to a
  fraction (default $0.1$) of the increment it produces, and the
  returned state is polished by one full-tolerance step. The
  validation is essential, not cosmetic: under a loose tolerance a
  warm-started solve can return \emph{unmoved}, faking a zero
  increment and false convergence; each step therefore checks its
  tolerance against the increment it measured and tightens and
  redoes (a warm continuation of the same solve) until the increment
  is resolved.
\item \textbf{An iteration budget} ($20\times$ the first step's
  count) stops escalated solves from burning thousands of iterations
  probing past the conditioning floor.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation.} \code{SetFluid} (the Maxwell-relaxation
overload, the front door's default treatment) on
\code{LinearQuasiStaticProblemBase}: because the per-step operator is
the gauge machinery's, every problem class inherits the mode through
its existing virtual solve --- the referential self-gravitating
class carries its potential block through unchanged --- in one
serial-and-parallel code path. The escalation updates the operator
by scaled sparse addition of a cached unit-penalty matrix (no finite
element reassembly per level). Validated against the exact
gravity-free Lam\'e solutions with a fluid core
(\code{tests/TestFluidGauge*}), and as architecture 5 of the seven in
\code{examples/self\_gravitating\_solvers}.

\paragraph{Cost (measured, degree-2 load).} On the two-dimensional
two-layer disc (order 2, $6474$ unknowns, serial, aw model): the
welded referential solve takes $1738$ MINRES iterations; the
relaxation $9910$ at $\beta_0 = 3$ and $3338$ at $\beta_0 = 30$
(converged, one operator); the slipping architectures $3700$--$4300$
(single-valued) and $17\,000$--$20\,000$ (broken-$\zeta$). On the
three-dimensional two-layer ball (order 1, $7874$ unknowns, 8 MPI
ranks): welded referential $425$ iterations; the relaxation $539$ at
$\beta_0 = 3$ and $\mathbf{314}$ at $\beta_0 = 30$ --- \emph{cheaper
than the welded static solve it replaces}, while handling the fluid
core with no slip machinery, no gauge refinements, and no fluid null
space. On \code{fluid\_core} the solver stops honestly at the
plateau (increment floor $7\times10^{-4}$), landing between the
welded and slip answers --- inside the band, as it must.

\paragraph{The one caveat.} The discrete limit is the
\emph{welded} limit: the fluid-relabelling suppression of the welded
space is inherited, not released (the welded--slip gap at
$N^2 \ne 0$ is gravitational, not elastic, so zero shear does not
remove it). The three-dimensional adjudication
(\code{love\_benchmark -method maxwell}): on the neutral
control \code{aw\_core} the degree-1 welded, slip and Maxwell
answers coincide to $6\times10^{-5}$ --- the suppression vanishes
with $N^2$, as it must; on \code{fluid\_core} the Maxwell answer
sits on the welded cluster at every degree, at degree 1
$1.77\,\%$ from Dahlen (welded $1.87\,\%$, the slipping pair
$2.0$--$4.2\,\%$), and its degree-1 relaxation stops at an
increment floor of $\sim10^{-2}$: the soft core--shell translation
is the band's own soft mode, and on a model with $|N^2|$ of order
the scale no full-elastic method resolves below the band there
(under $p$-refinement the welded answer itself moves $1.4\,\%$, the
Maxwell $0.2\,\%$). The right reading is not a defect but the
method's defining honesty: at $N^2 \ne 0$ the degree-1 static
response \emph{is} defined only to the band, every formulation
returns some point of it, and the relaxation is the one that reports
the fact in its stop diagnostics. For near-neutral (Earth-like)
cores the band closes and with it the caveat.

\section{Perspective, implications, and open items}
\label{sec:persp}

\paragraph{The status of the formulations.} Free slip is not physics
as such: it is the $\eta \to 0$ asymptotics of a viscous boundary
layer --- correct about what the \emph{interface} looks like once
the layer has collapsed, and silent about the \emph{interior}
relaxation that collapses it. The measurements agree: at
$N^2 \ne 0$ the slip classes wander in the same resolution band as
welded (freeing the interface kinematics does nothing for the
interior slaving), while Dahlen converges because its closure
contains \emph{both} halves of the dissipative limit, interface and
interior. The hierarchy is as follows. The Maxwell relaxation of the
fluid (\code{SetFluid}'s default treatment, \S\ref{sec:maxwell}) is
the general method: any geometry, any stratification, any number of
regions. The gauge-penalty treatment (\code{SetFluid} with
\code{GaugePenaltyOptions}) is clean only for neutrally stratified
fluids: at $N^2 \neq 0$ its semi-convergence is Rayleigh--Taylor
growth, and answers are defined only to the stratification band. For
hydrostatic (spherical) models Dahlen's mixed formulation, the
converged closed form of the dissipative limit, is the practical
workhorse. The \textbf{welded} and \textbf{slip} formulations are
finite idealisations of the dissipative completion --- one
representing the layer within a continuous space, the other
replacing it by its zero-width limit --- and serve as instruments:
the slip machinery measures the relabelling decomposition (the
welded-suppression question of \S\ref{sec:maxwell}), and the
welded gauged classes are the relaxation's own per-step engine.
At $N^2 = 0$ all of this collapses to a single answer, which is the
standing null test. Section~\ref{sec:general} develops a candidate
general static method --- Dahlen's closure extended to hydrostatic fluid
regions on any geometry, with one one-dimensional unknown per fluid
region --- whose standing against the relaxation waits on that
section's open steps and on the stable-core results.

\paragraph{The band, the secular state, and the Maxwell route.} The
elastic-static band is a property of the displacement formulations, not
of the physics. The secular state is unique, and every dissipative
completion reaches it (\S\ref{sec:regular}); the band is the spread of
the answers that formulations tying the fluid's normal trace to the
solid's return about it. The implemented Maxwell relaxation is, in its
limit, such a formulation: its discrete fixed point is the welded
static solve (\S\ref{sec:maxwell}, fact (i)), which relaxes the
fluid's deviatoric stress but keeps $u_r$ continuous at $\Sigma$. At
$N^2 \neq 0$ on spherical models it therefore returns the welded point
of the band rather than the secular closure --- about 1--2\,\% at
degree 1 on the non-neutral cores of the benchmark ladder
(\code{doc/benchmark\_programme.tex}, tier E2). This is a limitation of
the route, and is stated as such: in its implemented, welded form the
relaxation does not deliver the secular state when $N^2 \neq 0$. Its
value lies where Dahlen's route does not exist: at degree 0, where the
reduction leaves a free function (Step 1 of \S\ref{sec:slaving}), and
in aspherical geometry (\S\ref{sec:dahlen}).

\paragraph{Timescales.} The secular regime is entered when the
loading timescale exceeds the buoyancy timescale. Even a core
stratification as feeble as $N^2 \sim 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s}^{-2}$ ---
six orders below commonly discussed outer-core values --- gives
buoyancy periods of order years, against GIA loading periods of
millennia: $s/N \lesssim 10^{-3}$ in the most unfavourable
reading, and $\sim 10^{-7}$ for plausible values. For GIA, therefore,
the Dahlen closure is simply correct, the elastic-static band is
transient physics the Earth has long since relaxed through, and the
entire effect is negligible against (for example) three-dimensional
mantle-viscosity variations. The practical force of this note for GIA
is indirect: it explains the non-neutral resolution floor seen in
benchmark ladders, disciplines what comparisons against Dahlen-type
references (such as \code{pyslfp}) can and cannot resolve, and
identifies the neutral twin \code{aw\_core} as the configuration on
which everything is clean.

\paragraph{Where it is not academic.} Observables or bodies for which
core stratification is itself the target --- stably stratified layers
at the top of the core, other planets, or any inversion in which
$N^2$ is a parameter --- inherit an $N^2$-controlled modelling choice
that is invisible in standard treatments because the closure is
implicit. There the honest statement is: the static response is
defined only up to the secular closure, Dahlen computes that closure,
and the distance to any finite-time (partially relaxed) state is a
transient that requires the dissipative physics to evaluate.

\paragraph{Relation to the dynamic problem.}
The static pathology has a dynamic counterpart (a sketched argument of
the author's, not reproduced here). Qualitatively: for $N^2 \neq 0$ the
buoyancy coupling between the acoustic and internal sectors --- which
vanishes exactly in the neutral case --- gives the fluid operator an
essential spectrum touching the origin, so the static problem sits at
the edge of a continuum. With the coupling off, the kernel sector
decouples as pure gauge and the static quotient problem is well posed
(Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient}); with it on, the driving has no
static limit (Claim~\ref{claim:nonexist}, the non-uniform $s \to 0$
limit of \S\ref{sec:regular}), and the measurements of
\S\ref{sec:evidence} are numerical evidence for the dynamic picture as
well.
